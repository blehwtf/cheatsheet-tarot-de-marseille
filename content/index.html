--- title: "Tarot de Marseille — A Reader's Cheat Sheet" ---
01

Les Atouts — The Major Arcana

The 22 trump cards, numbered as they appear on a genuine Marseille deck (note the archaic IIII, VIIII, XIIII, XVIIII — Roman numerals as the 18th-century card makers actually cut them, not the "corrected" IV/IX/XIV/XIX you'll see in modern reprints). Card XIII was traditionally left unnamed. Each entry below reads the printed scene first, then what it tends to mean upright and reversed.

0
Le MatThe Fool

A ragged wanderer strides forward, stick over his shoulder, clothes torn to rags, while a small dog or wildcat bites at his leg from behind. He alone carries no number.

Folly · Freedom · The Wanderer
Upright
Movement without a fixed plan — a willingness to leave the map behind and trust the road itself. Liberation from convention, spontaneity, a fresh start unburdened by history. In a reading it often marks a choice made outside the "sensible" options.
Reversed
Aimlessness rather than freedom — wandering that has stopped going anywhere, carelessness, vulnerability from too little preparation, or mistaking chaos for spontaneity.
I
Le BateleurThe Magician

A street performer stands at a small table scattered with his tools — cup, coin, blade, and a wand raised in his right hand — echoing the four Minor suits in miniature.

Skill · Will · Resourcefulness
Upright
Everything needed for the next step is already on the table. Skill and improvisation over brute force; the resourceful use of what's at hand; the deliberate start of an undertaking. In work, an aptitude that counts for more than credentials.
Reversed
Sleight of hand without substance — trickery, a con, indecision dressed up as options, or talent left performing instead of producing.
II
La PapesseThe Popess

A seated woman in an abbess's habit holds a book open on her lap, a veil hanging behind her between two pillars — an unofficial counterpart to the Pope that scandalized early commentators.

Hidden Knowledge · Intuition · Patience
Upright
Knowledge held back on purpose — not everything is meant to be read at once. Intuition over declaration, quiet study, a truth still gestating. Often a signal to wait, listen, or trust a hunch rather than act.
Reversed
Withheld knowledge curdling into secrecy, superficial engagement with something that deserved depth, or a refusal to trust one's own intuition.
III
L'ImpératriceThe Empress

A crowned woman sits enthroned in the open air, scepter in hand, a shield bearing an eagle resting beside her — sovereignty exercised through generativity rather than force.

Fertility · Abundance · Nurture
Upright
Fertile authority — growth achieved by cultivating rather than commanding. Abundance, creative flow, nurturing that produces real results; the "pregnancy," literal or figurative, of an idea or relationship.
Reversed
Smothering care, creative block, fertility without follow-through, or dependency that has replaced healthy nurture.
IIII
L'EmpereurThe Emperor

Seated in profile, legs crossed in the same figure-four pose as the Empress, an eagle-marked shield at his side, one foot set forward as if paused mid-decision rather than static.

Authority · Structure · Order
Upright
Structure that holds weight. Earned authority, discipline, the architecture behind a stable outcome — strategic intelligence rather than raw ambition. A boss, a father figure, or one's own capacity to set firm terms.
Reversed
Authority curdled into control, rigidity that can't adapt, a structure maintained for its own sake, or power used to dominate rather than protect.
V
Le PapeThe Pope

Seated between two kneeling clerics, the Pope raises two fingers in blessing, a triple cross staff in his other hand — the composition mirrors an actual papal audience.

Tradition · Teaching · Blessing
Upright
Wisdom transmitted through a lineage — the value of listening to those who came before. Formal guidance, a mentor, a marriage or vow, counsel worth taking even when it isn't what you wanted to hear.
Reversed
Empty ritual, advice that no longer fits the situation, dogma enforced past its usefulness, or a refusal to accept any guidance at all.
VI
L'AmoureuxThe Lovers

A young man stands between two women — read as innocence and experience — visibly uncertain which way to turn, while a blindfolded Cupid draws his bow overhead.

Choice · Love · Temptation
Upright
A choice that can't be made by formula — something has to be felt through. Love, attraction, alignment of values, a decision where the heart casts the deciding vote, not just one option among many.
Reversed
A choice avoided, competing loyalties, a relationship out of balance, temptation pulling against better judgment.
VII
Le ChariotThe Chariot

A crowned figure rides a box-chariot pulled by two horses angled slightly away from one another — the driver holds no visible reins, controlling the tension by will alone.

Victory · Willpower · Momentum
Upright
Two pulls in different directions, held together and driven forward anyway. Determined progress, victory earned through control rather than force, a campaign that's finally moving, travel.
Reversed
The team pulling apart — loss of direction, forcing an advance past the point of control, burnout from holding too much together at once.
VIII
La JusticeJustice

A seated figure holds an upright sword in one hand and a balance scale in the other, facing forward with a level, unblinking gaze — weighing rather than judging from above.

Balance · Truth · Consequence
Upright
An honest accounting. Fairness, truth spoken plainly, a decision or contract weighed on its actual merits, consequences that land where they should.
Reversed
A thumb on the scale — bias, an unfair ruling, consequences dodged, or a truth avoided because it's inconvenient.
VIIII
L'HermiteThe Hermit

An old man in a hooded cloak walks with a staff, holding a lantern out in front of him — not to light a path already known, but to search for something not yet in view.

Withdrawal · Guidance · Prudence
Upright
Wisdom that only comes from stepping back. Solitude chosen on purpose, introspection, mentorship, patience with a slow process, prudence over speed.
Reversed
Isolation curdled into loneliness, excessive secrecy, or refusing guidance — the lantern raised but nobody willing to look where it points.
X
La Roue de FortuneWheel of Fortune

A hand-cranked wheel turns beneath a blindfolded or sphinx-like figure at its crest; one creature climbs the rising side while another tumbles down the falling side.

Cycles · Fate · Turning Point
Upright
A shift arriving on its own timetable. A turn of luck, a cycle completing, circumstances changing whether or not you feel ready — something long static finally moving again.
Reversed
Resistance to a turn that's coming anyway, a run of bad timing, or clinging to a position on the wheel that was never going to hold.
XI
La ForceStrength

A woman in a wide-brimmed, lemniscate-shaped hat calmly holds open the jaws of a lion, her expression untroubled — the contest, if it is one, is already decided.

Courage · Gentle Mastery
Upright
Power that doesn't need to shout. Composure under pressure, courage that outlasts force, mastering an impulse — one's own or someone else's — through patience rather than confrontation.
Reversed
Willpower giving way to impulse, self-doubt undermining real capability, or strength turned to aggression because patience ran out.
XII
Le PenduThe Hanged Man

A young man hangs by one foot from a gibbet of trimmed living branches, arms bound behind his back — his face is calm, and his free leg bends into a loose cross.

Suspension · Surrender · New View
Upright
A deliberate suspension. Letting go of forward motion on purpose, sacrifice made willingly, a problem finally solved by looking at it upside down instead of pushing harder.
Reversed
A pause that's become a stall, martyrdom performed rather than felt, resistance to a change that's clearly overdue.
XIII
L'Arcane sans NomDeath (unnamed)

A skeletal figure swings a scythe low across a field already scattered with severed hands, feet, and heads — yet fresh shoots keep sprouting from the same ground.

Transformation · Ending · Renewal
Upright
An ending that clears ground rather than just closing it. Necessary transformation, release of what no longer serves, the harvest that comes only after something has finished growing.
Reversed
Change refused, decay allowed to continue because ending it feels worse than enduring it, fear standing in for actual danger.
XIIII
TempéranceTemperance

A winged figure pours liquid between two vessels, one raised above the other, in a continuous unbroken stream — a blending that can't be rushed without spilling.

Moderation · Blending · Flow
Upright
Two things brought into proportion. Moderation, integration of opposites, healing that happens gradually, a plan that works because nobody is asking it to move faster than it can.
Reversed
Excess in one direction, incompatible elements forced together before they're ready, impatience that spills what patience was slowly combining.
XV
Le DiableThe Devil

A horned, winged figure stands on a pedestal above two smaller horned figures — chained at the neck, but loosely enough that the chain could be slipped if they tried.

Bondage · Temptation · Shadow
Upright
Naming what actually has a hold on you. Confronting habit, compulsion, material attachment, or a relationship built more on need than choice — the leash is real, but so is the slack in it.
Reversed
The chain finally tested — breaking a habit, facing down a fear once it's named, releasing an attachment that had outlived its purpose.
XVI
La Maison DieuThe Tower

Lightning strikes a tower and knocks its own crenellated crown loose, while two figures tumble from the height alongside falling debris — sudden and impossible to stop.

Upheaval · Collapse · Revelation
Upright
A structure collapsing because it was never actually sound. Disruption that forces honesty, a revelation that can't be unseen, upheaval that — however unwelcome — clears out something false.
Reversed
The same collapse delayed rather than avoided, or a crisis kept internal instead of open, doing quiet damage while looking, from outside, like nothing happened.
XVII
L'ÉtoileThe Star

A naked woman kneels by a pool, pouring water from two jugs — one back into the pool, one onto the land — under a sky of one large star and several smaller ones.

Hope · Inspiration · Renewal
Upright
Renewal after the Tower's collapse. Hope grounded in something real, quiet faith, a sense of purpose returning, generosity that costs nothing to give and helps regardless.
Reversed
Hope gone thin, disconnection from what used to feel inspiring, a wish held so tightly it turns into disappointment.
XVIII
La LuneThe Moon

Two towers flank a winding path to the horizon; a dog and a wolf howl up at a moon with a troubled human face, while a crayfish climbs from a pool below.

Illusion · The Unconscious · Doubt
Upright
Truths that surface slowly, and not always comfortably. Intuition working in the dark, dreams and anxieties both worth taking seriously, a situation that isn't yet what it will turn out to be.
Reversed
Confusion beginning to lift, a deception coming to light, or a fear that turns out to have been worse in imagination than in fact.
XVIIII
Le SoleilThe Sun

Two children stand close together beneath a large radiant sun, often before a low wall, unguarded and untroubled by it.

Vitality · Joy · Clarity
Upright
Nothing left to hide. Vitality, plain joy, a success visible enough that it doesn't need defending, clarity after long uncertainty.
Reversed
Joy delayed or clouded — by ego, by a setback that's more embarrassing than serious, or by success that hasn't been let in yet.
XX
Le JugementJudgement

An angel sounds a trumpet from the clouds; below, three figures — often read as a family — rise up out of an open tomb, called back not to be punished but accounted for.

Awakening · Reckoning · Rebirth
Upright
A call that has to be answered. Reckoning with the past in order to be free of it, a turning point recognized for what it is, renewal that follows honest accounting rather than avoidance.
Reversed
Self-judgment turned harsher than any outside verdict would be, or a summons ignored because facing it feels like too much right now.
XXI
Le MondeThe World

A dancer, draped only in a light scarf, moves within an oval wreath holding a wand in each hand, while the four fixed symbols of the tetramorph — angel, eagle, ox, lion — occupy its corners.

Completion · Wholeness · Fulfillment
Upright
Every piece finally in its place. Completion, integration of everything the previous twenty cards were working toward, a cycle that closes cleanly enough to become the ground for the next one.
Reversed
Something still outstanding — a project 90% finished, a sense of "almost," closure that's technically available but hasn't quite been claimed.
02

Les Quatre Séries — The Four Suits

The Minor Arcana (56 cards: ten pips and four court cards per suit) split into four domains, one per classical element. Where the Majors describe forces acting on a life, the suits describe the arena — which part of life a card's number and rank are actually playing out in.

BâtonsWands / Staves
Fire

The suit of will made visible — undertaking, ambition, the spark that turns an idea into a project. Bâtons cards read hot: fast to start, prone to friction, most alive when something is actually being built or fought for.

  • Career, enterprise, ambition
  • Energy, drive, momentum
  • Creative and entrepreneurial ventures
  • Conflict born of passion or competition

Weak: burnout, impulsiveness. Strong: initiative, courage to begin.

CoupesCups
Water

The suit of feeling — love, memory, imagination, and everything that moves a person from the inside rather than by argument. Coupes cards read slow and deep, carrying more than they show on the surface.

  • Love, romance, and connection
  • Mood, feeling, emotional truth
  • Intuition and imagination
  • Family, friendship, belonging

Weak: moodiness, avoidance. Strong: empathy, emotional honesty.

ÉpéesSwords
Air

The suit of the mind and its consequences — thought, argument, and the cut of a truth that can't be softened. Épées cards are the deck's most difficult run: not "bad" so much as unsparing about what's actually true.

  • Thought, argument, strategy
  • Conflict, struggle, rivalry
  • Communication and hard truths
  • Justice, consequence, decisiveness

Weak: cruelty, overthinking. Strong: clarity, decisive honesty.

DeniersCoins
Earth

The suit of the tangible — body, money, home, and whatever can actually be measured, held, or spent. Deniers cards move the slowest of the four, and reward patience more than any other suit.

  • Finances and material resources
  • Health and the physical body
  • Security, property, home
  • Craft, trade, practical skill

Weak: stinginess, stagnation. Strong: reliability, groundedness.

The reading below follows a widely used contemporary framework for those "hollow" pips: each number traces one stage of a plant's life cycle, from seed to scattered seed again, and every suit runs through the identical ten-stage arc in its own domain. Les Figures — Court Card Meanings

Four ranks × four suits = 16 court cards, the deck's people (or people-shaped moods). Read the rank for how the suit's energy shows up, the suit for where. One well-known modern framework maps the four ranks onto the three astrological modes — useful shorthand even if you don't read charts.

ValetPage — Cardinal mode

The suit's energy still learning its own shape: initiative, momentum, a beginning not yet tested. Cardinal energy instigates — it starts things rather than sustaining or adapting them.

CavalierKnight — Mutable mode

The most active court: pursuit, travel, a quest already underway. Mutable energy is adaptable and quick to change course — openness to whatever the road actually turns out to require.

ReineQueen — elemental blend

Mature receptivity built from two elements at once — the suit's own, plus the one that precedes it in the sequence. Wisdom and compassion held quietly, transmitted rather than performed.

RoiKing — Fixed mode

Authority, stability, resistance to change. Fixed energy holds its ground once it's been won — consistency and determination to maintain what the Page and Cavalier set in motion.

Les Couleurs — Pochoir Color Symbolism

Classic Marseille decks were block-printed in outline, then hand-colored through stencils (au pochoir) in a deliberately narrow palette — historically just four or five pigments per sheet, reused across all 78 cards. Paul Marteau's 1930s study of the deck went so far as to read color placement itself as part of a card's meaning: which figure is wearing red, which ground is painted yellow, what's left as bare paper. The table below gives each hue's core symbolic register.

ColorFrenchSymbolism
YellowJauneDivine light, spirit, and intellect — the highest, most illuminated tone on the card. Yellow grounds and backgrounds (as on Le Bateleur or Le Monde) mark a scene as touched by something beyond the purely material.
RedRougeVital force, action, blood, and will. Red is the deck's most active color — it dresses figures already in motion (Le Bateleur's sleeves, Le Chariot's canopy) and marks where a card's energy is doing something rather than resting.
BlueBleuSoul and interiority — the sky, the distance, the unconscious. Blue robes (La Papesse, La Vierge-adjacent figures) mark contemplation, receptivity, and things known inwardly before they're known outwardly.
GreenVertThe living, vegetal world — growth, hope, fertility. Green appears where a card's power is organic rather than built: foliage, the Hanged Man's living gibbet, the ground under L'Étoile.
FleshCarnationThe human body made present — tangible, mortal, physical reality. The pale flesh tone used for every bare face and hand is what keeps even the most symbolic cards anchored to an actual human figure.
BlackNoirThe woodblock outline itself — matter, boundary, the containing form. Every figure and object on a Marseille card exists inside a black line before it exists in color; black is structure, not absence.
White / bare paperBlancThe unmanifest — deliberately left unpainted where spirit is understood to show through matter, such as in eyes, small highlights, or the untouched paper ground behind a scene.

Reading Notes

  • Reversals are a later convention. The historical Marseille tradition was read upright only, drawing meaning from card combinations and position in the spread rather than orientation. Reversed meanings above are useful if that's the system you use, but treat them as an addition, not doctrine.
  • Read the numeral, not just the picture. Marseille decks lean on numerology and card position more than illustrative detail — a Marseille reader is often working the pattern (see Numbers, above) as much as the scene.
  • Card XIII stays unnamed on purpose in the historical decks. Some readers avoid saying "Death" aloud in a reading for the same reason — the blank is part of the card's weight.
  • Court cards can be people or postures. A Roi de Deniers might be an actual person in the querent's life, or simply "act with this kind of grounded authority right now."
  • Color reinforces number and suit, it doesn't override them. A card dominated by red intensifies whatever the number and suit already suggest — it's a modifier for tone, not a separate meaning layered on top.
  • The pip and court meanings above are one framework, not the only one. Because Marseille numeral cards carry no illustrated scene, there's no single "official" divinatory meaning printed on them — the plant-cycle stages and astrological court correspondences used here come from a well-regarded modern interpretive system; other Marseille readers build their pip meanings differently, and that's normal for this deck.
